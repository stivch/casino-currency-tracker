# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue about the code, not the person. Assume the other party is trying to help until
they give you a real reason to think otherwise.

## Scope

This applies to every space this project occupies — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code review, and the repository itself — and to any place where someone is
representing the project in public.

## Expected

- Give people the benefit of the doubt. Most friction here is a missing sentence of context, not
  malice.
- Keep criticism about the work: what it does, what it breaks, what it costs. "This misses the
  hidden-tab case" is review. "You clearly did not think" is not.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change that is perfectly good, because it does not fit
  the project. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) lists the settled positions so that this is
  rarely a surprise.
- Respect the subject matter. This project sits next to gambling, and some people reading it are
  using the limits and session tracking because they are trying to keep something under control.
  Do not joke about losses, do not tell anyone what they should bet, and do not treat the
  reporting features as a scoreboard.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private, including unwanted contact after being asked
  to stop.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or attacks based on who someone is — age, disability, ethnicity,
  sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, level of experience, education, income,
  nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual identity, or orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information — a real name, an address, an account identifier, a
  gambling history — without their explicit permission. This includes screenshots of another
  person's session.
- Deliberately posting someone else's credentials, session data, or account details, whether
  obtained through this project or not.
- Using the issue tracker to solicit, promote, or advertise: no affiliate links, no referral
  codes, no casino promotions, no "sponsored" tools.
- Sustained disruption — derailing threads, reopening settled decisions to relitigate them, or
  demanding unpaid work from a maintainer.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, or delete contributions that break these rules, and may warn,
temporarily block, or permanently ban anyone responsible. Enforcement is proportionate: a first
lapse usually gets a private word, and repeated or severe behaviour does not.

To report something, use GitHub's **Report abuse** on the content itself, open a private report
through the repository's **Security** tab if it involves someone's private data being exposed, or
email **steve1lion2@gmail.com**. Reports are handled discreetly and the reporter's identity is
not disclosed.

Maintainers who break these rules are subject to them like anyone else.
